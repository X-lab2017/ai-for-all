AI for All Manifesto

Make AI affordable, and useful.

Artificial intelligence (AI) is changing how people learn, create and solve problems. We advocate broader access to AI capabilities so that more people can put them to use, take part in creating and share in the benefits of technological progress.

OUR SHARED COMMITMENT

Inclusive access requires open, usable technology, people with the ability to create, communities that maintain shared work and sustained resources. Guided by a common vision, we bring these forces together: learning builds capability, contributions earn recognition and creation benefits more people.

One shared vision, two reinforcing forces, three flywheels, countless possibilities and continuous renewal. This framework connects our vision with action. Through shared practice, we refine our methods; through continuous improvement, we fulfill our commitments.

1
CHAPTER 01

One Shared Vision

Make AI affordable and useful.

Affordability means lowering the cost of obtaining and continuing to use technology, so that more people can access the tools, knowledge and resources they need. Usefulness means building the ability to understand, judge and create, so that technology truly serves learning, work and everyday life.

We put people first, using AI to expand human capabilities and choices. We advance this vision through three complementary perspectives.

For developers, support learning and creation. We support AI Learners in understanding technology and building skills, AI Builders in responding to needs and developing applications, and AI Engineers in improving systems and ensuring reliable operation.

For the wider public, improve everyday well-being. We bring technological achievements into daily life and work, helping people access knowledge, collaborate better and solve problems, so that more people can participate, use the results and benefit.

For the Global South, expand opportunities for development. We attend to differences in language, resources and development needs, respect local knowledge and practical experience, and work with local partners to build, use and maintain appropriate solutions.

All three perspectives serve AI for All. They consider both creators and users, supporting broad participation while responding to specific needs in different circumstances.

2
CHAPTER 02

Two Reinforcing Forces

Lower barriers through open source; expand possibilities through creativity.

Open and accessible technology enables more people to create. Developers bring learning, judgment and practical experience to bear on real needs. We help these two forces reinforce each other: openness supports creation, and creation enriches openness.

The power of open source lowers barriers to participation. From infrastructure and models to agents and applications, we support clear open licenses, understandable documentation, reusable work and ongoing maintenance, reducing the costs of accessing, learning and using technology.

The power of developers expands practical value. We encourage learning and practice grounded in real problems, valuing judgment, creativity and responsibility. Code, documentation, testing, teaching, translation, maintenance and community collaboration are all contributions worthy of respect.

Open-source projects and communities provide a shared space for these two forces to meet. Shared technology supports personal growth, while collaboration enriches tools and knowledge. Individual creativity contributes to shared work, and shared work enables further creation.

Our basic pathway is: AI capabilities → Digital public goods → AI for All. Developers apply available AI capabilities to real problems, creating and maintaining openly shared software, data, knowledge and solutions that help build digital public goods (DPG). Reuse, adoption and localization turn these results into widely accessible, practical benefits.

We value the openness, reusability and public value of digital public goods, support responsible development and use, and assess results against relevant standards. The value of public goods is realized through continued maintenance and practical application.

CHAPTER 03

Three Flywheels

Let data inform judgment, talent drive creation and value encourage investment.

Our three flywheels—data, talent and value—connect evidence, capabilities and resources. Evaluation improves practice; practice develops talent; verifiable outcomes help secure continued support. Together, these processes gradually form a mutually reinforcing system.

Data Flywheel

Collect data → Build benchmarks → Provide platform support → Apply evaluation, then use evaluation feedback to improve data collection.

We build on evidence from open-source practice, refine indicators and benchmarks, and develop platforms that support contribution recognition, collaboration analysis and outcome assessment. Building on existing work, we connect OpenDigger's data and indicators, OSIDX's exploration of indices and benchmarks, and OpenShare's platform practice, progressively linking data collection, benchmarking and evaluation.

Talent Flywheel

Evaluate contributions → Make growth visible → Bring talent together → Improve supply → Broaden benefits, then include new contributions and outcomes in the next evaluation.

We promote multidimensional contribution evaluation so that participants can see their growth and receive feedback, recognition and opportunities to practice. Fair rewards and incentives encourage more people to participate and help contributors keep growing. Learning, creation and maintenance improve the quality, usability and sustained provision of digital public goods, bringing practical help to more people.

Value Flywheel

Commit resources → Recognize contributions → Return value → Verify outcomes, then use the findings to guide the next round of investment.

We invite businesses and institutions to provide model-use credits, funding, jobs and practical opportunities that support developers and digital public goods, with clear resource conditions, scope and responsibilities. Combining OpenRank's collaboration-network evaluation methods with evidence from multiple dimensions of practice, we improve contribution assessment, talent identification and resource matching to make support more targeted. Judgments about capabilities and contributions should reflect their context and be tested in practice.

The three flywheels support one another: data provides evidence for evaluation and decisions; talent turns resources into capabilities and results; returned value helps secure continuing support for talent development, public-goods maintenance and data work. We test this cycle in practice and improve it as new evidence emerges.

CHAPTER 04

Countless Possibilities

Let openly shared results meet the diversity of human needs.

We bring tools, knowledge and solutions into learning, work and communities: helping beginners build skills, supporting developers in completing projects, helping organizations collaborate better, and supporting local partners in addressing their own language, cultural and public-service needs.

The impact of inclusion is reflected in what people actually gain. We continually ask whether results are affordable, understandable and useful; whether they expand capabilities and opportunities; whether they receive the maintenance they need; and who remains excluded by language, resources or barriers to use.

We respect differences between contexts and work with users and local partners to define needs, design solutions and assess outcomes, enabling more people both to create and to judge the value of what is created.

CHAPTER 05

Continuous Renewal

Make each round of practice the starting point for the next.

Through practice, documentation, evaluation and improvement, we continually turn our shared vision into action.

Start with needs and put ideas into practice. Open resources and opportunities for participation, support learning, projects and community collaboration, and clarify the goals, intended beneficiaries and scope of support for each action.

Keep public records and build evidence. Distinguish plans, pilots and completed work; document contributions, investment, user experience and changes in benefits; and state progress, conditions and limitations.

Involve multiple perspectives and assess outcomes. Combine data, user feedback and professional judgment, share results and problems openly, welcome differing views, and support scrutiny, correction and improvement.

Learn from experience and keep improving. Use new evidence to refine the data flywheel, new contributions and feedback to support the talent flywheel, and new outcome assessments to improve the value flywheel. Bring experience back into action and sustain the maintenance of useful results.
